# How to Play

`[review]`

*Star Legends – Galactic Wars* is played with percentile dice. Almost everything your character tries, from talking a customs officer out of a cargo inspection to deflecting a blaster bolt, comes down to one question: did you roll under your number? This chapter explains that question in full, then the things that hang off it: Success Levels, Advantage, Conditions, Wounds, fear, and how your character grows from a frightened farm kid on Tatooine into someone the galaxy has heard of.

Character creation is covered by [[SL100:Characteristics|Characteristics]], [[SL100:Species|Species]], [[SL100:Origin|Origin]], [[SL100:Faction|Faction]], [[SL100:Careers|Careers]], [[SL100:Skills|Skills]] and [[SL100:Talents|Talents]]; equipment is in [[SL100:Equipment|Equipment]], the Force in [[SL100:The Force|The Force]] and fighting in [[SL100:Combat|Combat]]. Everything here applies to all of them.

## The Core Loop

The Game Master (GM) describes the galaxy; the players say what their characters do; when the outcome is in doubt and matters, the dice decide. Most of a session is conversation. Dice come out when a character attempts something that could fail, where failure is interesting, and where the result is not obvious. A pilot does not Test to land a shuttle on a clear pad on a calm day. She Tests to land it on a storm-lashed Kamino platform with one engine out and a Republic gunship on her tail.

Time is handled in three ways. **Narrative time** is whatever the story needs: a week in hyperspace passes in a sentence. **Rounds** are used when the order of events matters, usually in combat: a Round is a few seconds in which every character gets a Turn. **Downtime** is the stretch between adventures, where characters train, earn, heal and pursue their own ends (see Between Adventures below and the Game Master page).

## Dice and Notation

**d100** means two ten-sided dice, one read as tens and one as units; 0 and 0 together is 100. **d10** is one ten-sided die, 0 reading as 10. Some rules ask for 1d10 Wounds or 2d10 + 20 for a characteristic.

Two tricks are used again and again. A **double** is a roll where both dice show the same number: 11, 22, 33, up to 00. A **reversed roll** swaps the two dice: a 37 reversed is 73, and this is how a hit location is found in combat without a second roll.

Any die roll may only be rerolled once, however many talents or points would let you try again.

## Tests

To make a Test, roll d100 and compare the result with your **Target**: the characteristic or skill being Tested, plus or minus any modifiers. A roll equal to or under the Target succeeds; over it fails. A roll of 01 to 05 always succeeds and 96 to 00 always fails, whatever the Target.

The ten characteristics are described in [[SL100:Characteristics|Characteristics]]. A skill is its characteristic plus the Advances you have bought in it, so a Smuggler with Fellowship 38 and 8 Advances in Charm has Charm 46. Basic skills (Athletics, Charm, Dodge, Perception and the rest marked *basic* on the [[SL100:Skills|Skills]] page) may be Tested on the bare characteristic even with no Advances. Advanced skills (Medicine, Pilot (Capital Ship), Slice Security System, Use the Force) cannot be attempted without at least one Advance; you simply do not know how. Grouped skills such as Lore, Ranged and Profession are bought one specialisation at a time; [[SL100:Skills|Skills]] lists them.

### Simple and Dramatic Tests

A **Simple Test** asks only whether you succeed: did the Bothan hear the patrol coming? Roll, compare, done.

A **Dramatic Test** asks how well. It produces **Success Levels** (SL): take the tens digit of your Target and subtract the tens digit of your roll. A Charm 46 against a roll of 23 is 4 − 2 = +2 SL. Against a roll of 61 it is 4 − 6 = −2 SL, a failure by two levels. A success always gives at least +1 SL and a failure at most −1 SL, even if the arithmetic says otherwise (Target 46, roll 41 is a success at +1 SL; roll 49 is a failure at −1 SL).

| SL | Result | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| +6 or more | Astounding success | Better than you hoped: the Hutt not only pays, he throws in a berth for the ship |
| +4 to +5 | Impressive success | A clear, clean win with something extra |
| +2 to +3 | Success | You did what you set out to do |
| 0 to +1 | Marginal success | Yes, but: it worked, with a complication or a cost |
| 0 to −1 | Marginal failure | No, but: it failed, with a consolation or a way forward |
| −2 to −3 | Failure | You did not manage it |
| −4 to −5 | Impressive failure | It went wrong in a way others will notice |
| −6 or less | Astounding failure | Disaster: the lock fuses, the Wookiee takes it personally, the alarm sounds |

The GM may use the **Fast SL** option: on any successful Test the SL is simply the tens die of the roll, which is quicker at the table and rewards high skill the same way on average.

### Difficulty

Before you roll, the GM sets a difficulty. Most Tests outside combat are Average (+20); combat is Challenging (+0). The scale is deliberately kind at the top: an Easy task should be easy.

| Difficulty | Modifier | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Very Easy | +60 | Bluffing a protocol droid that is programmed to be polite |
| Easy | +40 | Finding a cantina on Nar Shaddaa |
| Average | +20 | Haggling with a Jawa, slicing a civilian terminal |
| Challenging | +0 | Shooting a stormtrooper at short range, lying to a Jedi |
| Difficult | −10 | Slicing an Imperial Security Bureau datafile |
| Hard | −20 | Bluffing a Hutt crime lord on his own barge |
| Very Hard | −30 | Astrogating a blind jump without a navicomputer |

Situational modifiers (darkness, cover, haste, the right tools, the wrong atmosphere) stack with the difficulty, but the total modifier is never more than +60 or less than −30. A skill above 100 is still worth having: every full 10 points over 100 adds +1 SL to a successful Test.

### Doubles: Criticals and Fumbles

In combat, a successful attack or defence on a double is a **Critical**, which inflicts a Critical Wound (see Wounds and Injury, and [[SL100:Combat|Combat]]). A failed attack on a double is a **Fumble**: the GM rolls on the Oops! table in [[SL100:Combat|Combat]], which runs from dropping your weapon to shooting the nearest friend. The GM may extend this to any Test: a double on a success is then an Astounding Success whatever the SL, and a double on a failure an Astounding Failure. A blaster whose power pack is faulty, or a Force power channelled in anger, may carry its own consequences for doubles.

### Opposed Tests

When two characters work against each other, both Test and compare SL; the higher wins, and the margin between them is the **net SL** that drives the result. If the SL are equal, the character with the higher Target wins. Most combat is opposed: an attacker's Melee against a defender's Melee or Dodge, a Stealth (Urban) against the sentry's Perception, a Charm against an Intimidate. Modifiers normally apply to both sides unless one of them clearly earns the advantage alone.

### Extended Tests

Some tasks take time and several attempts: slicing a locked vault, repairing a hyperdrive under fire, tracking a fugitive across the Jundland Wastes. The GM sets a target SL (often 5 to 10) and a time per Test (a Round, a minute, a day). Each Test adds its SL to a running total; successes build it up and failures tear it down. Reaching the target completes the task. If the total ever falls below 0 it resets to 0, and the GM may rule that a particularly bad roll ends the attempt. For a simpler game, count successes as +1 and failures as −1 regardless of SL.

### Assisted Tests

Comrades can help. The best-skilled character makes the Test and gains +10 for each helper who has at least one Advance in the skill, is close enough to assist, and is not doing anything else. The number of helpers is limited by the roller's Characteristic Bonus for the Tested characteristic. Nobody can assist a Test made to resist something (a Cool Test against fear, an Endurance Test against poison).

### Group Tests

Sometimes a whole party Tests at once: everyone makes a Stealth (Rural) Test to slip past the Tusken camp, everyone makes a Cool Test when the rancor's gate rises. The GM decides whether one failure spoils the attempt (the sentry hears the clumsiest of you) or each character stands on their own result.

## Advantage

Combat and other confrontations have momentum, and *Star Legends* tracks it as **Advantage**. Each point of Advantage you hold gives +10 to your combat Tests and to Tests against fear and other Psychology. Advantage is gained and lost quickly:

**You gain +1 Advantage when you:** win an Opposed Test in combat; Charge; attack an opponent who is Surprised or Stunned; wound an opponent with a ranged attack; defeat an important enemy (+1 or +2 at the GM's discretion); or spend an Action sizing up the situation with a fitting skill (Intuition, Lore (Warfare), Outdoor Survival, Perception and similar), up to the relevant Characteristic Bonus.

**You lose all your Advantage when you** lose an Opposed Test in combat, suffer any Condition, or lose any Wounds. You lose 1 Advantage at the end of a Round in which you gained none, or in which you ended outnumbered. All Advantage is gone when the fight ends.

A character with more Advantage than any opponent engaging them may spend all of it to Disengage and walk away without a Test. A Leadership Test can pass Advantage to allies. Force powers are helped by Advantage; the long meditation of a channelled power is not ([[SL100:The Force|The Force]]). The GM may cap Advantage at the character's Initiative Bonus, or at a flat value, if runaway momentum is making fights one-sided.

## Fortune, Resolve and Destiny in Play

[[SL100:Characteristics|Characteristics]] explains where these points come from. In play they work like this. **Fortune** is renewed at the start of each session to your Destiny score. Spend one to reroll a failed Test, to add +1 SL to a Test after rolling, or to choose where in the Initiative order you act this Round. **Resolve** is renewed whenever you act on your Motivation. Spend one to ignore fear and all other Psychology for a Round, to ignore the modifiers of your Critical Wounds for a Round, or to remove one Condition (removing Prone also heals 1 Wound). **Destiny** and **Resilience** are permanent and precious. A Destiny point burned keeps you alive when you should have died, either by removing you from the scene unconscious or by ignoring the blow entirely. A Resilience point burned lets you refuse a pull of the dark side ([[SL100:The Force|The Force]]) or declare that you will not fail: you set the dice to whatever you want, or win an Opposed Test by +1 SL, even after the roll.

## Movement and Time in Rounds

When the GM calls for Rounds, characters act in order of Initiative, highest first, Agility breaking ties (or by one of the rolled-initiative options in [[SL100:Combat|Combat]]). On your Turn you have one **Move** and one **Action**, in either order, plus any number of Free Actions that need no Test: drawing a blaster, shouting a warning, dropping prone, keying a comlink.

Your Move carries you up to Walk (2 × Movement metres) in any direction. **Running** uses your Action as well and takes you Run (4 × Movement metres); an Average Athletics Test adds SL more metres. Climbing, swimming and crawling halve your pace unless a skill or talent says otherwise. Jumping, falling and other stunts are in [[SL100:Combat|Combat]].

## Conditions

Conditions describe things that have gone wrong with you. Most give −10 to Tests they would sensibly affect and have a way to shake them off. Conditions of the same kind stack; different kinds do not. Gaining any Condition wipes your Advantage. When you remove the last Condition of a stack you are left with 1 Fatigued, unless the Condition removed was Fatigued itself.

| Condition | Effect | Removing it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ablaze | 1d10 Wounds a Round, +1 per extra Ablaze, ignoring Toughness Bonus; armour protects only if it is sealed | Athletics Test as an Action, or someone smothers the flames; removed by water, vacuum or fire-suppressant |
| Bleeding | Lose 1 Wound a Round per Bleeding, ignoring everything; at 0 Wounds, 10% chance per Bleeding each Round of dying | Medicine Test (removes 1 + SL), medpac or bacta patch; resting removes 1 a Round if you do nothing else |
| Blinded | −10 to sight Tests; −10 per Blinded to Melee and Ranged; Ranged beyond Point Blank impossible | Clears at the end of the Round after the Round you gained it, unless the source persists |
| Broken | You must flee the enemy and may do nothing else; −10 to all Tests | Cool Test when out of sight of enemies, or at the end of a Round you ended in cover |
| Deafened | −10 to hearing Tests, −10 per Deafened to Initiative | Clears at the end of the Round after the Round you gained it |
| Entangled | Cannot Move; −10 per Entangled to Melee; Ranged impossible | Opposed Strength Test against the net, cable or grapple; a vibroblade cuts most bonds |
| Fatigued | −10 to all Tests per Fatigued | Rest: one removed per hour of quiet, or per good night's sleep, or by Meditate |
| Poisoned | −10 to all Tests and lose 1 Wound per Poisoned each Round; at 0 Wounds, 10% chance per Poisoned each Round of dying | Endurance Test at the end of each Round (removes 1 + SL), antidote, or bacta |
| Prone | Can only crawl; +20 to be hit in melee, −20 to be hit by ranged fire beyond Point Blank; −20 to Melee | Spend your Move to stand |
| Stunned | No Action, and you defend at −10; opponents gain +1 Advantage when attacking you | Endurance Test at the end of each Round (removes 1 + SL) |
| Surprised | No Move or Action, cannot defend; attackers gain +20 and +1 Advantage | Ends at the end of the Round, or after the first attack on you |
| Unconscious | Helpless; any hit is a Critical Wound | Wakes after Toughness Bonus Rounds if at 1+ Wounds, or when healed above 0 |

The GM will invent Conditions for the setting as needed: **Ionised** for droids and powered armour hit by ion weapons (as Stunned, but cleared by an Average Profession (Mechanic) Test or a restart), **Irradiated** for exposure near a damaged reactor (as Poisoned, cleared only by medical care), **Suffocating** in vacuum or poison atmosphere (Fatigued each Round, then 1d10 Wounds a Round when Fatigued equals Toughness Bonus).

## Wounds and Injury

Your Wounds ([[SL100:Characteristics|Characteristics]]) are your reserve of punishment. Losing Wounds carries no penalty until they run out; a hero at 1 Wound fights as well as at full. When an attack hits, its Damage is the weapon's Damage plus the attacker's SL; Wounds lost are Damage minus your Toughness Bonus minus the Armour Points at the location struck, to a minimum of 1 (stun and other Undamaging weapons have no minimum). [[SL100:Combat|Combat]] gives the full combat procedure, hit locations and the lightsaber's Resist rule.

**At 0 Wounds** you are knocked Prone and, after Toughness Bonus Rounds, fall Unconscious unless healed. Any further damage while at 0 Wounds inflicts a Critical Wound.

**Critical Wounds** come from a Critical (a double on a successful attack), from damage that would take you below 0 Wounds (the overflow decides how badly: if the overflow is less than your Toughness Bonus, roll on the Critical table at −20), and from certain weapon qualities. Reverse the attack roll for the location and roll d100 on that location's Critical table in [[SL100:Combat|Combat]]. The result gives a fixed Wound loss that ignores armour and Toughness, plus an injury: Conditions, a broken bone, a torn muscle, a severed limb, or on a 00, death. Critical Wounds stay with you until treated.

**Death.** If you are Unconscious at 0 Wounds with more Critical Wounds than your Toughness Bonus, you die at the end of the Round unless someone heals at least one of them. A helpless character can simply be killed by anyone who takes the time. Destiny is the last word: a player may burn a point to walk away from certain death.

**Healing.** Each day, after a night's sleep, make an Average Endurance Test and recover SL + Toughness Bonus Wounds (minimum 0); a full day of rest adds Toughness Bonus more. The Medicine skill restores Intelligence Bonus + SL Wounds to a patient once per encounter and treats Bleeding and Poisoned. A **medpac** adds +10 to the Medicine Test and prevents infection; a **bacta patch** heals 1d10 Wounds over an hour; a **bacta tank** heals Toughness Bonus × 2 Wounds per hour of immersion and clears one Critical Wound per day of treatment. Surgery (a medical droid or a Medicine Test with the Surgery talent) is needed for the Critical results that say so. Broken bones mend in 30 + 1d10 days (a failed Endurance Test afterward leaves −5 permanently to the characteristic concerned), torn muscles in 30 − Toughness Bonus days, and a lost limb is lost unless replaced by a cybernetic ([[SL100:Equipment|Equipment]]).

The GM may use the **Sudden Death** option for unnamed enemies: a stormtrooper or a Gamorrean guard reduced to 0 Wounds simply falls.

## Fear, Rage and the Mind

Some things shake even hardened spacers. **Psychology** Tests are Cool Tests, and a success normally holds for the rest of the encounter. Advantage adds +10, and a point of Resolve buys a Round of immunity.

**Fear (n).** A rancor, a Sith Lord, a dianoga rising from the water. Make an Extended Cool Test to n SL. Until you reach it you suffer −1 SL on all Tests against the source and must pass a Cool Test to approach it, or become Broken. **Terror (n).** Worse than fear: a krayt dragon, a Dark Lord unleashing the Force, the floor of a Geonosian arena. Make one Cool Test; on a failure you gain n plus your negative SL Broken Conditions, and then treat the source as Fear (n).

**Hatred (X).** Wookiees for Trandoshan slavers, a Rebel for the Empire that glassed her homeworld. You must attack the hated thing if you can and gain +1 SL on Tests to harm it; you are immune to Fear it causes. **Animosity (X)** is the lesser form: −20 to Fellowship Tests involving the target unless you act on your dislike. **Prejudice (X)** costs −10 to Fellowship Tests unless you make your contempt clear.

**Frenzy.** Berserkers, Wookiees whose family is threatened, dark-side adepts giving in to rage. Make a Willpower Test to enter Frenzy (or to avoid it when provoked): you are immune to Psychology, must Charge the nearest enemy, make a free Melee Test every Round, add +1 to Strength Bonus for damage, and gain a Fatigued Condition when it ends.

The GM can build other traits from the same pieces: a **Phobia** is Fear (1) of something specific; **Trauma** forces a Cool Test when a memory is triggered; **Loyalty** gives +1 SL defending a person or cause and a Cool Test to abandon them. Species notes in [[SL100:Species|Species]] list traits that come with a people.

## Status and Standing

Every career level carries a Status: a tier (Fringe, Citizen or Elite, written Brass, Silver and Gold in the current career lists until the renaming is applied) and a Standing from 1 to 5. Status is who will talk to you and how. Charm gains +10 against a lower tier and −10 against a higher; Intimidate gains +10 against a lower tier; Gossip is −10 across tiers; Leadership gains +10 per tier you stand above those you command. Travelling incognito you count as the lowest tier, Standing 3.

Status must be maintained. Living to your Standing costs roughly half your Standing in the tier's coin each day: a few credits for the Fringe, tens of credits for Citizens, hundreds for the Elite. Fall short and you lose 1 Standing per week; at Standing 0 you drop a tier. Earning is an Endeavour (see below): a week of work with your career's Earning skill at Average difficulty brings in the tier's base per point of Standing, half on a failure and nothing on a disaster. The credit values for each tier are in [[SL100:Equipment|Equipment]].

## Experience and Advancement

The GM awards Experience Points (XP) at the end of each session: typically 50 to 150 for the session, with more for finishing an adventure, for good play and for ambitions achieved. A short-term Ambition fulfilled is worth 50 XP, a long-term one 500 (or the player may retire the character and carry half its XP into a new one). Party ambitions pay the same to everyone.

XP buys **Advances** in characteristics, skills and talents. Advances inside your current career level and the levels below it cost the standard rate; anything outside your career costs double, and non-career talents can only be learned through Endeavours.

| Advances already held | Characteristic | Skill |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 to 5 | 25 | 10 |
| 6 to 10 | 30 | 15 |
| 11 to 15 | 40 | 20 |
| 16 to 20 | 50 | 30 |
| 21 to 25 | 70 | 40 |
| 26 to 30 | 90 | 60 |
| 31 to 35 | 120 | 80 |
| 36 to 40 | 150 | 110 |
| 41 to 45 | 190 | 140 |
| 46 to 50 | 230 | 180 |
| 51 to 55 | 280 | 220 |
| 56 to 60 | 330 | 270 |
| 61 to 65 | 390 | 320 |
| 66 to 70 | 450 | 380 |
| 71 or more | 520 | 440 |

Each Advance in a characteristic raises it by 1; each Advance in a skill raises the skill by 1. A **talent** costs 100 XP for its first rank, 200 for the second, 300 for the third, up to its Max. Talents may only be bought from your current career level.

**Completing a career level.** A level is complete when you have at least 5 (level 1), 10 (level 2), 15 (level 3) or 20 (level 4) Advances in every characteristic the level opens, the same number of Advances in eight of its skills, and at least one of its talents. Advances bought earlier count. A complete level is a badge: it shows in your trappings, your Status and how others treat you.

**Changing career** costs 100 XP from a complete level or 200 from an incomplete one. You may step up to the next level of your own career (or back down to a lower one), start level 1 of any career in your class, or move to the same level of a career in another class if the story justifies it, for 100 XP more. You never have to move; a Smuggler can stay a Smuggler all her life. Events in play (a knighting, a court-martial, a Jedi Master taking you as a Padawan) may move you without XP at the GM's call.

## Between Adventures

Downtime is spent in **Endeavours**, one per free week up to three between adventures. The common ones are: **Income** (work your Earning skill); **Training** (learn a non-career characteristic or skill at career cost, plus a tutor's fee); **Unusual Learning** (a non-career talent, needing a Hard Test, +10 for each previous failure); **Crafting** (an Extended Profession Test with materials at a quarter of the price); **Commission** (order an Exotic item from a craftsman); **Consult an Expert** (a Gossip Test to find one, a Favour to pay, and a reroll on a related Test in the next adventure); **Banking** (a holonet account that pays d10% interest and may not be there when you come back); and **Change Career**. The GM may also roll on the Events table, where Imperial audits, old enemies, cut purses and unexpected inheritances happen to the party in their absence. Money not spent, banked or hidden tends to vanish between adventures: spacers live large. Endeavours by class, Favours and the Events table are on the Game Master page.

## Quick Reference

- **Test:** d100 ≤ characteristic or skill + modifier. 01–05 always succeeds, 96–00 always fails.
- **SL:** tens of Target − tens of roll. Success ≥ +1, failure ≤ −1.
- **Difficulty:** Very Easy +60, Easy +40, Average +20, Challenging +0, Difficult −10, Hard −20, Very Hard −30. Total modifiers between +60 and −30.
- **Opposed:** higher SL wins, ties to the higher Target; net SL is the margin.
- **Extended:** accumulate SL to a target; below 0 resets to 0.
- **Assisted:** +10 per helper with an Advance, up to the roller's Characteristic Bonus.
- **Double in combat:** success = Critical, failure = Fumble.
- **Advantage:** +10 per point; gained by winning, charging, wounding; lost entirely on losing, any Condition or any Wound.
- **Wounds lost:** Damage (weapon + SL) − Toughness Bonus − Armour Points, minimum 1.
- **0 Wounds:** Prone, Unconscious after TB Rounds; further hits are Criticals; death when Criticals exceed TB.
- **Healing:** sleep heals SL + TB; Medicine heals IntB + SL once per encounter; bacta patch 1d10 in an hour.
- **Fear (n):** Extended Cool to n SL. **Terror (n):** one Cool Test, fail = n + |SL| Broken.
- **XP:** talents 100 per rank; characteristics and skills by the table; non-career double; change career 100 or 200.
