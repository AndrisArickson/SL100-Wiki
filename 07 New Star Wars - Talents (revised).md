# Talents

Talents are the knacks, training and quirks that set one character apart from another. Each talent lists a **Max**, the number of times it may be taken (a number, or a Characteristic Bonus that rises as the character grows), and where it applies, the **Tests** it is tied to. A talent tied to Tests also adds +1 SL to successful Tests of that kind for every rank taken, on top of its written rule. Talents are bought from the current level of a character's career for 100 XP, then 200 XP for a second rank, 300 XP for a third, and so on. Talents outside the career can only be learned through an Endeavour (see Endeavours).

Six entries are marked *Placeholder*: they anticipate chapters not yet written (starship combat, medical rules, the tradition pages) and are there to remind us those chapters are owed.

A note on literacy: in Star Legends the **Datapad Use** talent stands in for reading, writing and basic computer use. Almost everyone raised on a civilised world has it; only characters from feral, feudal or otherwise isolated origins start without it, and until they learn it they can neither read text nor work a datapad, terminal or holonet interface.

## General Talents

### Accurate Shot
**Max:** Ballistic Skill Bonus

You know exactly where a blaster bolt or slug needs to land to put a target down, whether that is the gap in a stormtrooper's plastoid or the soft joint of a battle droid. Add your level in Accurate Shot as extra Damage to every successful hit you make with a ranged weapon.

### Ace Pilot
**Max:** Agility Bonus · **Tests:** Pilot Tests in a chase or dogfight

*Placeholder until the starship and vehicle combat chapter is written.* You fly on instinct when the shooting starts, and the ship answers. When you fail a Pilot Test during a chase, a dogfight or an evasive manoeuvre under fire, you may reverse the dice if that scores a success. The Pilot talent covers hazards of space; this one covers the enemy.

### Acute Sense (Sense)
**Max:** Initiative Bonus · **Tests:** Perception (Sense)

One of your five senses is far keener than normal, letting you pick up on details nobody else registers. The GM may allow you Perception Tests to notice things that would ordinarily be impossible to detect with that sense: spotting a speeder bike beyond the range of anyone else's eyes, catching the faint scent of a nerve toxin in a drink, hearing a mouse droid idling behind a bulkhead, feeling a worn inscription on a Jedi holocron, or tasting that two casks of Corellian ale came from different vats.

### Alley Cat
**Max:** Initiative Bonus · **Tests:** Stealth (Urban)

You grew up in the undercity, where the lower levels of Coruscant or the back alleys of Nar Shaddaa are home and the shadows are old friends. When you fail a Stealth (Urban) Test, you may reverse the dice of the roll if doing so would turn it into a success.

### Ambidextrous
**Max:** 2

Through long practice or natural gift, your off-hand is nearly as capable as your main one. Tests that rely solely on your secondary hand suffer only a -10 penalty rather than the usual -20. If you take this talent a second time, you suffer no off-hand penalty at all.

### Amphibious
**Max:** 1

Like a Mon Calamari or a Quarren, you are as much at home beneath the waves as above them. You can breathe both air and water without difficulty. Swim always counts as a career skill for you, no matter what career you are in, and each Advance in it costs you 5 XP less than normal.

### Animal Companion
**Max:** 1

A creature has bonded to you and goes where you go: an akk dog, a tooka, a Kowakian monkey-lizard, a trained hawk-bat. The companion has its own stat block (see the Bestiary) and acts on its own turn, obeying simple commands; it may assist any Test its senses or instincts bear on for +10, and while it is within sight you gain +1 SL on successful Perception and Intuition Tests to notice danger. If the companion dies, a new bond takes an Endeavour. Droid Companion is the mechanical equivalent.

### Argumentative
**Max:** Fellowship Bonus · **Tests:** Charm Tests when arguing and debating

You relish a good dispute and have a knack for coming out on top, whether facing a Senate committee or a cantina loudmouth. When you succeed at a Charm Test to debate or argue with an opponent, you may use either the SL you rolled or the number shown on your units die as your result. A successful roll of 24, for instance, could count as +4 SL.

### Artistic
**Max:** Dexterity Bonus · **Tests:** Profession (Artist)

You have a true eye for form and line, and given time and suitable media you can produce accurate and expressive work. This has many practical uses, from drawing up a likeness for a bounty posting to keeping an illustrated record of a survey, with spot benefits at the GM's discretion. In addition, add Profession (Artist) to any career you enter; if the skill is already part of that career, each Advance instead costs you 5 XP less.

### Attractive
**Max:** Fellowship Bonus · **Tests:** Charm Tests to influence those attracted to you

Striking eyes, an easy smile, a confident bearing: whatever it is, you know how to make the most of your looks. When you succeed at a Charm Test to influence someone who is attracted to you, you may use either the SL you rolled or the number on your units die. A successful roll of 38 could therefore count as +8 SL.

### Battle Rage
**Max:** Willpower Bonus · **Tests:** Melee Tests when Frenzied

You have learned to ride your Frenzy rather than be carried away by it, like a Wookiee berserker who can still recognise friend from foe. At the end of any Round, you may attempt a Cool Test; if it succeeds, your Frenzy ends.

### Beat Blade
**Max:** Weapon Skill Bonus · **Tests:** Melee for a Beat Blade

You have drilled in striking your opponent's weapon with short, controlled blows that knock it out of line, opening them up or spoiling their next attack. Before rolling, you may declare that your Action is a Beat Blade. Make a Melee Test; if you succeed, your opponent loses 1 Advantage, plus a further 1 Advantage for every SL you score. The Test is not Opposed. Beat Blade is useless against an opponent who has no weapon or who is of a larger Size than you (see Combat: Size).

### Beneath Notice
**Max:** Fellowship Bonus · **Tests:** Stealth when in plain sight

To the powerful you are just another servant, labourer or droid-minder, not worth a second glance. Provided you are dressed appropriately and not somewhere you obviously should not be, characters of a higher Status Tier will normally ignore you until your presence becomes awkward, which makes overhearing an Imperial officer's private conversation remarkably easy. Furthermore, anyone of a higher Status Tier than you gains no Advantage for striking or wounding you in combat; beating someone so lowly brings them no credit.

### Berserk Charge
**Max:** Strength Bonus · **Tests:** Melee on a Round when you Charge

You throw your whole body behind a charge, letting momentum do the work of your blows. On any Round in which you Charge, add +1 Damage to all your Melee attacks for each level you have in this talent.

### Blather
**Max:** Fellowship Bonus · **Tests:** Charm to Blather

Spacers call it "venting the airlock", Hutt-space traders call it "selling sand to Jawas": a torrent of rapid, endless, meaningless talk designed to leave the listener utterly bewildered. Attempt an Opposed Charm/Intelligence Test against your target. If you win, the target gains a Stunned Condition, plus one additional Stunned Condition for each level you have in Blather. While Stunned in this way, the target can do nothing but stare at you, trying to follow what you are saying. When the last Stunned Condition ends they finally get a word in, and they may be far from pleased. If you stop talking, all Stunned Conditions caused by your Blather end at once. You can normally only Blather at a given character once per scene, or longer at the GM's discretion, as the target quickly learns to tune you out.

### Blood Feud
**Max:** None · **Tests:** Any Test to find, follow or fight the subject of your feud

A particular being, family or clan wronged you, and you have not forgotten. Each time you take this talent name the subject of a feud. You gain +1 SL on successful Tests to track, confront or fight them and their close kin or lieutenants, and their Intimidate, Fear and Terror have no hold on you. You must pass a Challenging (+0) Cool Test to let them walk away when you could end it. Mandalorian clan feuds, Hutt vendettas and the bounty hunter who never let a mark go all run on this talent.

### Bookish
**Max:** Intelligence Bonus · **Tests:** Research

You are never happier than when surrounded by data: the Jedi Archives, a university's holonet records, or a dusty cache of flimsiplast in an old Republic outpost. When you fail a Research Test, you may reverse the dice of the roll if doing so would turn it into a success.

### Break and Enter
**Max:** Strength Bonus · **Tests:** Melee when forcing or breaking inanimate objects

You know how to put a blast door, a cargo crate or a security hatch out of commission in a hurry. Add +1 Damage for each level you have in this talent when rolling Damage against inanimate objects such as doors, windows, lockers, containers and the like.

### Briber
**Max:** Fellowship Bonus · **Tests:** Bribery

You know exactly how much a customs official, a dock warden or a stormtrooper sergeant needs to look the other way, and it is always less than they first ask. The GM reduces the base cost of any bribe you need to pay by 10% for every level you have in Briber, to a minimum of 10% of the original amount.

### Cadence
**Max:** Intelligence Bonus · **Tests:** Entertain (Sing)

You know the work-songs, marching cadences and spacer chants that keep a crew moving as one: the hauling chants of Wookiee shipwrights, the cadence calls of clone and stormtrooper columns, the sabacc-table songs of Corellian freighter crews. Each rank is one cadence learned. Establishing one takes thirty seconds and a successful Entertain (Sing) Test; it then affects every ally who can hear you for three minutes plus SL minutes, giving the crew +10 to one kind of group Test named by the cadence (hauling, rowing a lifeboat, marching, repairs under fire). While you lead it you may only Dodge; taking Damage or failing an Opposed Test ends it. One cadence per watch.

### Captain's Word
**Max:** Fellowship Bonus · **Tests:** Leadership aboard your own ship or with your own crew

Your crew trust you, and it shows when the shooting starts. Any Leadership Test made aboard your ship or involving your own crew gains +1 SL per rank, and the bonus applies to crew Tests (gunnery, damage control, boarding) as well as to individual orders. The Word is only as good as the ship: a captain who loses the vessel loses the bonus until the crew sails under them again.

### Cardsharp
**Max:** Intelligence Bonus · **Tests:** Gamble and Sleight of Hand when playing games of chance

You have spent many nights at the sabacc and Pazaak tables, and you usually walk away ahead, though not always honestly. When you succeed at a Gamble or Sleight of Hand Test while playing cards, you may use either the SL you rolled or the number on your units die; a successful roll of 28 could count as +8 SL. If the group plays an actual card game to represent what is happening at the table, you may be dealt extra cards equal to your level in Cardsharp each deal, then discard down to the normal hand size before each round of play.

### Careful Strike
**Max:** Initiative Bonus

Whether firing or swinging, you land your blows where you intend rather than where chance puts them. You may shift your Hit Location result by up to +10 or -10 for each level you have in this talent. With two levels, a hit on location 34 (Right Arm) could be moved down to 14 (Left Arm) or up to 54 (Body).

### Carouser
**Max:** Toughness Bonus · **Tests:** Charm at parties, Gossip at parties, Consume Intoxicant

You are the life of every cantina and the last one standing at every celebration, whether it is a Corellian spacer's wake or a Hutt's palace feast. When you fail a Consume Intoxicant Test, you may reverse the dice of the roll if doing so would turn it into a success.

### Cat-tongued
**Max:** Fellowship Bonus · **Tests:** Charm when lying

Like a veteran smuggler talking their way past a customs inspection, you weave truth and fiction together so seamlessly that no one can tell them apart. When you use Charm to lie, your listeners may not oppose your Test with their Intuition to sense that something is wrong with your story.

### Catfall
**Max:** Agility Bonus · **Tests:** Athletics when falling

You have the balance and instincts of a born climber and can drop from heights that would cripple others. Whenever you fall, make an Athletics Test. If you succeed, reduce the distance fallen by 1 metre, plus a further 1 metre for every +1 SL scored, when calculating the Damage you take.

### Clan Markings
**Max:** 1 · **Tests:** Intimidate and Cool while your markings are displayed

*Placeholder until the Faction and tradition pages are written.* Your armour colours, war paint, tattoos or scarification announce who you are and who stands behind you. While they are visible you gain +10 to Intimidate Tests against anyone who knows what they mean and +10 to Cool Tests against Fear and Terror; those who hate your clan gain the same bonus to Intimidate you. Tusken war paint, Mandalorian clan colours and Sith tattoos are all Clan Markings.

### Combat Aware
**Max:** Initiative Bonus · **Tests:** Perception during melee

You keep a constant eye on the whole fight, reading its shifts and surges rather than fixing on a single enemy. You may attempt a Challenging (+0) Perception Test to ignore being Surprised; the Test is modified by circumstances as normal.

### Combat Master
**Max:** Agility Bonus

Years of fighting have taught you how to hold a crowd of lesser opponents at bay, like a Jedi Knight surrounded by battle droids. For each level you have in this talent, you count as one additional person when working out whether one side outnumbers the other (see Combat: Outnumbering). This only applies when you are the one being outnumbered.

### Combat Reflexes
**Max:** Initiative Bonus

Your reactions are startlingly quick, and you are moving before most people realise a fight has started. Add +10 to your Initiative for each level you have in this talent when determining Combat Initiative.

### Commanding Presence
**Max:** Fellowship Bonus · **Tests:** Leadership

You carry an air of authority that makes others fall quiet and wait for your instructions, like a Grand Moff stepping onto a bridge. Characters of a lower Status than you may not resist your Leadership Tests with their Willpower. Your enemies are no more inclined to obey you than before, but ordinary folk rarely stand in your way.

### Concoct
**Max:** Intelligence Bonus · **Tests:** Lore (Pharmacology)

You can mix stimulants, antidotes and remedies from whatever is to hand, without a proper lab. You may take one free Crafting Endeavour to use Profession (Pharmacist) without needing a workshop. Any further Crafting Endeavours follow the usual rules (see Endeavours).

### Contortionist
**Max:** Agility Bonus · **Tests:** Perform and Agility Tests when contorting helps

You can fold, twist and compress your body in ways that look impossible, which lets you slip through ventilation shafts, cargo gaps and sets of binders alike. The GM decides what benefit this gives in a given situation, and may call for a successful Agility Test.

### Coolheaded
**Max:** 1

Nothing seems to rattle you, not even a Sith Lord's glare. You gain a permanent +5 bonus to your starting Willpower Characteristic. This increase does not count towards your Advances.

### Crack the Whip
**Max:** Dexterity Bonus · **Tests:** Ride or Pilot Tests when Fleeing or Running

You know how to squeeze every last bit of speed from a dewback, a swoop or a battered landspeeder. Whenever a mount or vehicle under your control is Fleeing or Running, it gains +1 Movement.

### Craftsman (Profession)
**Max:** Dexterity Bonus · **Tests:** Profession (any one)

You have a gift for one particular craft, whether that is armoursmithing in the Mandalorian tradition, droid repair or starship engineering. Add the associated Profession skill to any career you enter. If that Profession skill is already part of the career, each Advance in it instead costs you 5 XP less than normal.

### Creature Affinity
**Max:** Willpower Bonus · **Tests:** Creature Training

Creatures sense something in you that puts them at ease, and they will often follow your lead. Any creature with the Bestial trait that has not been trained to be aggressive automatically stays calm in your presence unless it is given a reason not to be. Banthas, tauntauns, dewbacks, varactyls, massiffs, nexu and akk dogs are all examples of such creatures.

### Criminal
**Max:** None

You make your living from illegal sources, and it shows: gang tattoos, a shifty manner, a certain roughness around the edges, whatever fits. Other characters treat you as one Status Tier lower than you actually are, unless they also have the Criminal talent, in which case Status is compared as normal. Law enforcement, from local constables to the ISB, is always suspicious of you and your motives, and this only grows worse with each additional level, the exact consequences set by the GM. Lawbreakers without this talent earn far less from their crimes, but nothing marks them out and they keep their Status. With the GM's consent, you may remove levels of Criminal by paying the same XP they cost to buy.

### Datapad Use
**Max:** 1

You can read, write and operate datapads, computer terminals, holo-displays and the holonet in every language you can speak. Nearly every citizen of a civilised world has this talent, and it replaces any other form of literacy. Only characters from uncivilised origins, such as feral upbringings, feudal cultures or isolated backwater worlds, begin play without it. A character who lacks Datapad Use cannot read written text or operate a computer interface until they learn it.

### Deadeye Shot
**Max:** 1

Right between the eyes, or wherever else you meant to put it: your shots go exactly where you choose. Instead of reversing the dice to determine the Hit Location struck by your ranged weapons, you may simply pick the location.

### Dealmaker
**Max:** Fellowship Bonus · **Tests:** Haggle

You could sell a used YT-1300 to a Hutt and have them thank you for the privilege. When you use the Haggle skill, you reduce or increase the price in question by an extra 10%. The GM may set a lower limit on prices to represent a seller refusing to go below cost.

Each time you take this talent, you gain bonus money of +2d10 x 10 credits.

### Diceman
**Max:** Intelligence Bonus · **Tests:** Gamble and Sleight of Hand when playing dice games

You are a master of chance cubes and every other dice game played from Mos Eisley to Nar Shaddaa, and anyone who says you cheat is obviously mistaken. When you succeed at a Gamble or Sleight of Hand Test while playing dice, you may use either the SL you rolled or the number on your units die; a successful roll of 06 could count as +6 SL. If the group plays a real dice game to represent what is happening in-game, always roll extra dice equal to your level in Diceman and keep the best results.

### Dirty Fighting
**Max:** Weapon Skill Bonus · **Tests:** Melee (Brawling)

You learned to fight in the alleys and spaceport bars where there are no rules, only winners. Whenever you land a successful Melee (Brawling) hit, you may choose to inflict an extra +1 Damage for each level you have in Dirty Fighting. Using this talent counts as cheating in any formal bout, such as a shockboxing match or an arena contest.

### Disarm
**Max:** Initiative Bonus · **Tests:** Melee Tests concerning this Talent

A deft twist of your weapon or a sharp blow to the hand sends your opponent's weapon spinning away. For your Action, attempt an Opposed Melee/Melee Test. If you win, your opponent drops a held weapon, which flies 1d10 metres in a random direction, with any further effects decided by the GM. If you win by 2 SL, you choose how far the weapon travels instead of rolling; if you win by 4 SL, you also choose the direction; and if you win by 6 SL or more, you may snatch the weapon from the air with a flourish, provided you have a free hand. Disarm is useless against an opponent who has no weapon or who is of a larger Size than you (see Combat: Size).

### Distract
**Max:** Agility Bonus · **Tests:** Athletics to Distract

You know how a sudden feint of the body, a thrown glance or a scuffed boot can pull an opponent's attention away from what you are really doing. You may spend your Move to attempt a Distraction, resolved as an Opposed Athletics/Cool Test against one opponent. If you win, that opponent cannot gain Advantage until the end of the following Round. A Mos Eisley brawler might kick dust before the real blow lands.

### Drilled
**Max:** Weapon Skill Bonus · **Tests:** Melee Tests when beside an ally with Drilled

You have drilled to fight in formation, holding a line with comrades rather than as a lone duellist. Whenever an enemy causes you to lose Advantage while you are standing beside an active ally who also has the Drilled Talent, you may keep 1 of the lost Advantage for each level you have in Drilled. Clone troopers and Rebel line infantry both train this way.

### Droid Companion
**Max:** 3

A droid travels with you and works as your partner rather than your property. The droid has its own stat block (see the Bestiary) chosen by rank: at rank 1 a simple unit such as a mouse droid, probe or pit droid; at rank 2 an astromech, protocol or medical droid; at rank 3 a custom or military-grade model with two programmed skills. The droid may assist any Test that falls within its programming for +10 without the usual limit on helpers, and may act on its own turn using one of its skills at your Intelligence. If the droid is destroyed, a replacement takes an Endeavour and its purchase price.

### Dual Wielder
**Max:** Agility Bonus · **Tests:** Melee or Ranged when attacking with two weapons

Armed with a weapon in each hand, you may attack with both as a single Action. Roll to hit with your primary-hand weapon and resolve it as normal, keeping the dice roll. If it hit, your secondary weapon may then attack any available opponent using that same roll reversed: a 34 becomes a 43. Apply your off-hand penalty (–20 unless you have the Ambidextrous Talent) to this second roll. It is Opposed by a fresh defending roll and its Damage is worked out normally. If your first strike scored a Critical, the number rolled on the Critical table is used as the to-hit roll for the second attack instead. Choosing to attack with both weapons gives all your defensive rolls a –10 penalty until the start of your next Turn, and you only gain Advantage from striking or Wounding while Dual Wielding if both attacks hit.

### Duct Crawler
**Max:** Initiative Bonus · **Tests:** Stealth (Underground)

You learned to move through the places a city forgets: maintenance shafts, ventilation ducts, the undercity levels of Coruscant, the warrens of Sullust, the tunnels of a spice mine. When you fail a Stealth (Underground) Test you may reverse the dice if that scores a success.

### Eidetic Memory
**Max:** Intelligence Bonus

Your mind files away sequences of facts, codes and names with near-perfect fidelity. You can recall a sequence of items up to a length equal to your Intelligence without a Test. For every further 10 items you try to memorise, the recall requires an Intelligence Test of increasing difficulty, starting at Very Easy (+60) for the first extra 10, Easy (+40) for 20, Average (+20) for 30, and so on. This is clearly useful for Gamble Tests, where the GM may grant +20 to +60 depending on how much memorising sequences matters to the game, and the GM may apply bonuses to other Tests as appropriate. Each level of the Talent lets you hold one extra sequence without forgetting one already stored.

### Embezzle
**Max:** Intelligence Bonus · **Tests:** Intelligence (Embezzling)

You quietly skim credits from whoever is paying you, keeping the books clean enough that nobody notices. Whenever you receive money through Earning, whether during play or from an Income Endeavour, you may attempt an Opposed Intelligence Test against your employer (if you have one). If you win, you pocket (2d10 + SL) credits, (1d10 + SL) x 10 credits, or (1 + SL) x 1,000 credits, depending on the size of the operation as decided by the GM, without being caught. If your employer wins by 6 or more SL, you still get the money but your theft is discovered, with consequences left to the GM. Any other result means you fail to skim anything.

### Enclosed Fighter
**Max:** Agility Bonus · **Tests:** Dodge in enclosed environments

You fight comfortably in cramped quarters: starship corridors, maintenance shafts, trash compactors and crowded cantina back rooms. You ignore any penalties to Melee caused by confined spaces such as tunnels, a packed front line, narrow fighting pits and the like, and you may use the Dodge Skill even when limited space would normally forbid it.

### Etiquette (Social Group)
**Max:** Fellowship Bonus · **Tests:** Charm and Gossip (Social Group)

You know the manners, phrases and unspoken rules of a particular social circle and can pass as one of its own, provided you dress and behave the part. Example groups: Criminals, Imperials, Rebels, Hutts' courts, Nobles, Scholars, Servants, Soldiers, Spacers, Trade Guilds, Jedi. Those who have the Talent for a group will spot the awkwardness of anyone who lacks it. This is mostly a roleplaying matter, though the GM may grant a bonus to Fellowship Tests where it applies.

### Fast Hands
**Max:** Dexterity Bonus · **Tests:** Sleight of Hand, Melee (Brawling) to touch an opponent

Your hands move faster than most eyes can follow. Bystanders receive no passive Perception Tests to notice your use of the Sleight of Hand Skill; they may only Oppose your Sleight of Hand Tests if they actively suspect you and are watching for your movements. In addition, any attempt to use Melee (Brawling) simply to touch an opponent gains a bonus of +10 x your level in Fast Hands.

### Fast Shot
**Max:** Agility Bonus · **Tests:** Ranged when making a Fast Shot

You draw and fire before anyone else has reacted. If you hold a loaded ranged weapon, you may fire it outside the normal Initiative order, before any other combatant acts in the coming Round, rolling to hit with all the usual modifiers. Doing so uses both the Action and the Move of your upcoming turn, which count as spent when that turn arrives. If two or more characters use Fast Shot, the one with more levels in the Talent fires first; if they are equal, the shots are simultaneous and resolved together. Every cantina gunslinger argues about who shot first.

### Fearless (Enemy)
**Max:** Willpower Bonus · **Tests:** Cool to oppose your Enemy's Intimidate, Fear, and Terror

Whether through courage or sheer recklessness, one particular kind of enemy no longer frightens you. On encountering the specified enemy, a single Average (+20) Cool Test lets you ignore any Intimidate, Fear or Terror effects it produces. Typical choices include stormtroopers, bounty hunters, Imperial officers, Tusken Raiders, Wookiees, Sith and rancors.

### Feint
**Max:** Weapon Skill Bonus · **Tests:** Melee (any) for Feints

You have practised the false attack: a lunge or swing meant to deceive rather than wound. For your Action you may Feint against any opponent who is using a weapon, resolved as an Opposed Melee (any)/Melee Test. If you win and you attack that same opponent before the end of the next Round, you may add the SL of your Feint to your attack roll.

### Field Dressing
**Max:** Intelligence Bonus · **Tests:** Medicine during combat Rounds

You patch people up fast while blaster fire is still flying. If you fail a Medicine Test while using a medpac or bacta patch, you may reverse the roll if that would turn it into a success. When you do, you may score no more than +1 SL, since you have traded care for speed.

### Field Medic
**Max:** Intelligence Bonus · **Tests:** Profession (Medic)

You are an expert in battlefield pharmacology: stimpacks, injector ampoules, bacta gel, anti-venoms and whatever else keeps a soldier moving. You may reverse any failed Profession (Medic) Test if doing so allows the Test to succeed.

### First Strike
**Max:** Initiative Bonus · **Tests:** Melee Tests when you act before your opponent in a Round

You strike while others are still deciding. When you make a Melee attack against an opponent who has not yet acted this Round, you gain +1 Advantage before rolling, once per Round. Echani duellists and lightsaber masters of Form II prize this above almost anything else.

### Flee!
**Max:** Agility Bonus · **Tests:** Athletics when Fleeing

With your life on the line, you find a burst of speed that surprises even you. Your Movement Attribute counts as 1 higher when Fleeing (see Combat: Fleeing).

### Fleet Footed
**Max:** 1

You are naturally quick on your feet, covering ground faster than others of your kind. You gain +1 to your Movement Attribute.

### Frenzy
**Max:** 1

Something in you snaps when the fighting starts, and you give yourself over to a red, roaring fury. You can enter Frenzy (see Psychology). The Frenzy Talent is added to the Talent list of any career you are in. A Wookiee berserker or a rage-driven Sith acolyte might take this path.

### Frightening
**Max:** Strength Bonus

There is something about you that makes sensible beings keep their distance. If you wish, you have a Fear Rating of 1 (see Psychology). Add +1 to this rating for each additional level of the Talent. A scarred Trandoshan bounty hunter or a hulking Gamorrean enforcer might carry this reputation.

### Furious Assault
**Max:** Agility Bonus · **Tests:** Melee when making extra attacks

Your attacks come one after another in a relentless storm. Once per Round, if you hit an opponent in close combat, you may immediately spend an Advantage or your Move to make one extra attack (if using your Move, you must still have it available).

### Gregarious
**Max:** Fellowship Bonus · **Tests:** Gossip Tests with travellers

You enjoy chatting with strangers, and they tend to open up to you in return, whether in a spaceport cantina or on the long haul between systems. You may reverse any failed Gossip Test if doing so allows the Test to succeed.

### Gunnery Chief
**Max:** Initiative Bonus · **Tests:** Ranged Tests with crewed weapons

*Placeholder until the starship and vehicle combat chapter is written.* You know how to get a gun crew working as one. Any crewed weapon you direct, from a tripod repeater to a capital ship's turbolaser battery, adds +1 SL per rank to its successful Ranged Tests, and the crew needs one fewer member than the weapon's listed minimum, to a minimum of one.

### Hardy
**Max:** Toughness Bonus

### Strong-Minded
**Max:** Willpower Bonus

Your will is iron, and you refuse to be worn down. Add your level in Strong-Minded to your maximum Resolve pool. Imperial interrogators have broken many prisoners, but not those with this kind of determination.

### Sturdy
**Max:** Strength Bonus · **Tests:** Strength Tests when lifting

You have a heavy frame, or have simply spent years hauling cargo. Increase the number of Encumbrance Points you can carry by your level in Sturdy x 2. Dockworkers at Mos Eisley spaceport and Wookiee labourers put this to use daily.

### Suave
**Max:** 1

You have an easy manner that puts others at their ease. You gain a permanent +5 bonus to your starting Fellowship Characteristic (this does not count towards your Advances).

### Super Numerate
**Max:** Intelligence Bonus · **Tests:** Evaluate, Gamble

Numbers come to you as naturally as speech, and you can work through most calculations in your head. You may use a simple calculator to represent what your character is capable of computing mentally. Sabacc sharks and Hutt bookkeepers rarely lack this gift.

### Supportive
**Max:** Fellowship Bonus · **Tests:** Social Tests to influence a superior

You know how to pitch a request so that those above you listen. When you successfully use a social Skill to influence someone of a higher Status Tier, you may choose to use either your rolled SL or the number showing on your units die. A successful roll of 46, for instance, could be taken as +6 SL. Ambitious Imperial junior officers get a great deal of mileage from this.

### Sure Shot
**Max:** Initiative Bonus

You find the gaps in a target's protection and put your shots there. When you hit a target with a Ranged weapon, you may ignore Armour Points equal to your level in Sure Shot. Veteran rebels learn quickly where stormtrooper plating is weakest.

### Surgery
**Max:** Intelligence Bonus · **Tests:** Medicine Tests outside combat rounds, when you have time to work properly

You are trained to open and close living tissue to save a life. You can treat any Critical Wound marked as needing Surgery. You can also operate to resolve internal injuries with an Extended Challenging (+0) Medicine Test, with a target SL set by the GM (usually 5-10) according to the difficulty of the procedure. Each Test causes 1d10 Wounds and 1 Bleeding Condition, so a careless surgeon can easily kill the patient. After surgery, the patient must pass an Average (+20) Endurance Test or gain a Minor Infection (see Disease and Infection). On most worlds a medical droid and a bacta tank are the usual alternative, but where neither is available this talent is all that stands between a patient and death.

### Surgical Precision
**Max:** Intelligence Bonus · **Tests:** Medicine Tests during surgery

*Placeholder until the medical and healing rules are written.* Your hands are as steady as a medical droid's. Reduce the Wounds inflicted by each Test of a surgical procedure by your rank, and if you score +4 SL or better on a surgery Test the patient suffers no Bleeding Condition from it. Requires the Surgery talent.

### Tactician
**Max:** Intelligence Bonus · **Tests:** Leadership

You read a battlefield the way others read a datapad. At the start of a combat in which you can see the ground and your allies can hear you, you may take a Challenging (+0) Leadership Test; on a success your side gains Advantage equal to your rank, which you distribute among your allies as you see fit. Once per Round you may also spend your Move to give one ally who can hear you +10 to their next Test. Clone commanders, Rebel cell leaders and Mandalorian field marshals are Tacticians.

### Tenacious
**Max:** Toughness Bonus · **Tests:** Endurance Tests for enduring hardships

You do not quit, however hopeless the ordeal. You may double the length of time a successful Endurance Test lets you endure a hardship, including long rides, exposure, gruelling rituals and similar trials. Those who have crossed the Jundland Wastes on foot know what this means.

### Tinker
**Max:** Dexterity Bonus · **Tests:** Profession Tests to repair broken items

You can coax almost anything back into working order with the right tools and a little patience. You count all Profession Skills as Basic when repairing broken items. Jawa scavengers and freighter mechanics keeping an ageing YT-1300 flying are rarely without this talent.

### Trapper
**Max:** Initiative Bonus · **Tests:** Perception Tests to spot traps and mines, Demolitions

You have an eye for snares, tripwires and buried mines, and know how to lay your own. You may take a Perception Test to spot traps automatically, without having to tell the GM you are looking; the GM may prefer to make some of these Tests on your behalf in secret. Ewok hunters and Rebel commandos share this instinct.

### Trick Riding
**Max:** Agility Bonus · **Tests:** Dodge Tests on ridden creatures

You are as nimble on the back of a beast as you are on the ground. You can use any of your Perform Skills and your unmodified Dodge Skill while mounted on a creature. In addition, when mounted, you may make your Move at the start of the Round instead of on your Turn. Tusken Raiders on banthas and Rebel scouts on tauntauns make full use of this.

### Unshakeable
**Max:** Toughness Bonus · **Tests:** Endurance Tests to keep your footing

You stand like a durasteel bulkhead. Blasts, shoves, charging banthas and the push of the Force all struggle to move you. Each rank adds +1 SL to Tests to resist being knocked Prone, pushed back, thrown or moved against your will, including Opposed Tests against Force powers that would move you.

### Vacuum Legs
**Max:** Toughness Bonus · **Tests:** All those taken to resist Space-Sickness

The lurch of a hyperspace jump and the drift of low gravity no longer trouble you. You can ignore any penalties to Tests made to avoid Space-Sickness.

### Very Resilient
**Max:** 1

You shrug off punishment that would floor most beings. You gain a permanent +5 bonus to your starting Toughness Characteristic (this does not count towards your Advances).

### Very Strong
**Max:** 1

You are far stronger than you look. You gain a permanent +5 bonus to your starting Strength Characteristic (this does not count towards your Advances).

### Vice (Target)
**Max:** Willpower Bonus · **Tests:** Willpower Tests to resist the chosen vice

You have a weakness and you know it: spice, death sticks, sabacc, Corellian whiskey, risk itself. Choose the vice when you take this talent. Each day you go without it you must pass a Willpower Test or suffer −10 to all Tests until you indulge. When you do indulge, you may regain 1 Resolve, once per session per rank. Taking the talent more than once deepens the same vice or adds another.

### War Leader
**Max:** Fellowship Bonus · **Tests:** Leadership Tests in battle

Your voice carries over blaster fire and your presence steadies the line. All subordinates who can see you may add your level in War Leader to their SL in one Willpower Test per Round. This bonus does not stack. Rebel field commanders and Imperial sergeants alike hold their troops together this way.

### Warrior Born
**Max:** 1

You took to close combat as if you were made for it. You gain a permanent +5 bonus to your starting Weapon Skill Characteristic (this does not count towards your Advances).

### Wealthy
**Max:** None

You have money to burn and are seldom short of credits. When Earning (including Income Endeavours), you secure an additional 1,000 credits for each level of Wealthy you have. Core World nobility and Hutt crime lords rarely feel the pinch.

### Well-Prepared
**Max:** Initiative Bonus

You think ahead, and your pockets always seem to hold what the moment needs. A number of times per session equal to your level in Well-Prepared, you may produce the Trapping required for the current situation from your pack (or similar), provided it is Encumbrance 0, could plausibly have been bought recently, and does not stretch credibility too far. This could be a stimpack, a spare power cell, a glow rod or a ration bar. Whenever you do this, deduct the item's cost from your credits, representing the purchase you made earlier.

## Force Talents

There are two ways to touch the Force in Star Legends, and the talents below are split accordingly.

**Disciplines.** Jedi, Sith and most other traditions train the three classic disciplines: Sense, Control and Alter. Each discipline is a ranked talent. Every rank is +1 SL on successful Use the Force Tests of that discipline and shows how deeply the character has trained in it. Taking a discipline talent for the first time grants its universal powers at once; these are used with a plain Use the Force Test, have no Casting Number and no XP cost, and work like a trained reflex. Named powers beyond the universal ones are bought with Force Specialty. Powers of this kind are never spells: a failed Test simply does nothing, and the only price is paid in Dark Side Influence when a power is used in anger or to dominate.

**Sorcery.** A few traditions, the Sith sorcerers, the Nightsisters and Witches of Dathomir, the Krath and their like, reach the Force through incantation, ritual and alchemy. Their talents use the sorcery rules (Language (The Force) to cast, Use the Force to channel, Miscasts on doubles, rituals that take hours) and are listed at the end of this section.

### Force Sensitive (Tradition)
**Max:** 1

The Force is strong with you, and a tradition has taught you to listen to it. Choose one tradition when you take this talent (Jedi Order, Sith, Jensaarai, Matukai, Baran Do, Zeison Sha, Dathomiri, or another agreed with the GM); you may only ever belong to one. Use the Force is added to every career you enter, and you may now buy the Force Sense, Force Control and Force Alter talents. Your tradition decides which discipline it teaches first, whether it leans to the light or the dark, and which powers its Tradition Adept talent unlocks. When you spend a Fortune point to reroll a failed Test you may instead call on the Force and reduce the original roll by 1d10.

### Force Sense (Discipline)
**Max:** Initiative Bonus · **Tests:** Use the Force (Sense)

You feel the living Force around you: the pull of danger before it arrives, the warmth of life behind a bulkhead, the taint or purity of a place. Each rank is +1 SL on successful Use the Force (Sense) Tests. On taking the first rank you gain the universal Sense powers: Danger Sense (a warning of imminent threat; you may ignore Surprise on a successful Test), Life Detection (feel living beings through walls or darkness within Willpower Bonus × 10 metres) and Sense Force (judge the strength and the light or dark alignment of the Force in a place or object).

### Force Control (Discipline)
**Max:** Willpower Bonus · **Tests:** Use the Force (Control)

You have learned mastery over your own body and the Force that flows within it. Each rank is +1 SL on successful Use the Force (Control) Tests. On taking the first rank you gain the universal Control powers: Force Speed (your Movement counts as doubled for one Round per SL), Reduce Injury (on a hit, reduce incoming Damage by SL), Concentration (ignore the effects of one Fatigued or Stunned Condition and all Fear for the scene), Absorb Energy (disperse heat, radiation or electrical damage over time, halving it) and Tapas (resist cold and, with Force Shield, survive briefly in vacuum).

### Force Alter (Discipline)
**Max:** Fellowship Bonus · **Tests:** Use the Force (Alter)

You reach out and shape the galaxy beyond yourself: matter, energy and other minds. Each rank is +1 SL on successful Use the Force (Alter) Tests. On taking the first rank you gain the universal Alter powers: Telekinesis (push, pull or lift an object of Encumbrance up to SL × Willpower Bonus within Willpower Bonus × 10 metres), Affect Mind (plant a simple suggestion in a weak mind with an Opposed Use the Force/Willpower Test; it never works on the strong-willed or the forewarned), Kinetic Combat (add SL to the Damage of one melee strike this Round) and Force Shield (a bubble of Force that turns aside blaster bolts and slugs; count as having a shield with the Shield (SL) quality until your next Turn).

### Force Specialty
**Max:** None · **Tests:** Use the Force (the power's discipline)

Each time you take this talent you learn one named Force power you qualify for; the disciplines it requires are shown below. Specialty powers are used with a Use the Force Test in the relevant discipline, gaining the discipline's rank bonus, and may be improved with +2 SL in the usual way (longer duration, more targets, greater reach). The GM may require an instructor, a holocron or a tradition to teach a given power. Unlike other talents, every rank of Force Specialty costs a flat 100 XP.

*Alter powers:* Projective Telepathy (send thoughts or images to a known mind at any distance), Force Dominate (an Opposed command the target struggles to disobey; a dark act), Force Lightning (arcs of destructive energy, Damage SL + Willpower Bonus, Ablaze on a Critical; always dark), Force Illusion (convincing sensory hallucinations).

*Control powers:* Accelerate Healing (recover Willpower Bonus + SL Wounds after an hour's meditation), Force Hibernation (slow the body to near death to survive starvation, vacuum or grave injury), Detoxify Poison (purge venom or drugs from your own body), Force of Will (reinforce your mind against domination, interrogation and intrusion; +2 SL to resist).

*Sense powers:* Farsight (see distant places or glimpses of possible futures), Postcognition (read the impressions left in an object or place), Sense Personal Force (gauge the strength and alignment of the Force in another being), Receptive Telepathy (read surface thoughts and emotions), Beast Languages (understand the intent of creatures and calm them).

*Combined powers* need two or three disciplines working together:

| Power | Disciplines | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Battle Meditation | Sense + Control + Alter | Allies in sight add your SL to Cool and Leadership Tests; enemies subtract it. The signature power of Nomi Sunrider. |
| Force Meld | Sense + Control + Alter | Link several Force users into one shared awareness; all share the best Initiative and ignore Surprise. |
| Tutaminis | Control + Alter | Absorb raw energy, including Force Lightning and a single blaster bolt per Round, harmlessly. |
| Force Cloak | Control + Alter | Bend light and sound around yourself; +SL × 10 to Stealth until you act. |
| Force Healing (Others) | Sense + Control + Alter | Heal another by Willpower Bonus + SL Wounds, or begin the healing of one Critical Wound. |
| Plant Surge | Sense + Alter | Drive explosive growth in plant life; entangle a target or raise cover. |
| Cleanse Soul | Control + Alter | Spend one of your own Resilience points to remove SL Dark Side Influence from another. |

### Tradition Adept
**Max:** 1

You have gone deep into the teachings of your own tradition rather than the broad path. You may learn your tradition's signature powers (Jedi Battle Precognition, Sith Rage, Matukai body techniques, Zeison Sha telekinetic combat, and so on, as written up for each tradition) for 100 XP each, as if they were Force Specialty powers, without needing to qualify through a combined-discipline requirement. You must have Force Sensitive (Tradition).

### Force Sight
**Max:** Initiative Bonus · **Tests:** Any Test to perceive the currents of the Force

You perceive the Force directly, as a Miraluka does, seeing the glow of life and the eddies of the dark side where others see only matter. You may use Perception, Intuition or Track to perceive Force phenomena, Force users and the residue of Force powers, and you suffer no penalty for darkness or blindness when sensing living things.

### Force Visions
**Max:** Initiative Bonus · **Tests:** Intuition Tests at a Force nexus

The Force shows you what has passed. You always know when you stand on a Force nexus or vergence, such as the caves of Dagobah or the Valley of the Dark Lords, and may take an Intuition Test to receive a vision of significant events that took place there. Visions are often obscure and coloured by your tradition's view of the Force.

### Detect Force Artefact
**Max:** Initiative Bonus · **Tests:** Intuition Tests to detect Force artefacts

You can tell when the Force lingers in an object: a holocron, a Sith amulet, a Jedi relic, an unusual kyber crystal. A successful Intuition Test reveals that the item is Force-attuned, and each further SL reveals one of its properties, whether it leans to the light or the dark, and roughly how old it is.

### Force Fury
**Max:** Fellowship Bonus

Your powers carry the anger you feel for your enemies. You deal +1 Damage per rank with any Force power that causes Damage. Each time you use this bonus the GM may award Dark Side Influence, for the Force answers rage with rage.

### Pure Soul
**Max:** Willpower Bonus

Your spirit is anchored to the light, and darkness finds little purchase on it. You may hold a number of extra Dark Side Influence points equal to your rank in Pure Soul before you must Test to see whether you have fallen (see Force Influence).

### Foretold
**Max:** 1

When you were young, a seer of the Force drew you aside and told you how you would die. Agree a suitable foretelling with the GM. Should your character die in a manner that matches the vision, your next character begins play with a bonus of half the XP your dead character earned during play.

### Force Resistance
**Max:** 1

The Force slides off you like rain from a duracrete wall. You may Oppose any Force power that targets you with a Willpower Test, exactly as a trained Force user resists a power, even if you have no training at all. You may never learn Force Sensitive (Tradition), Force Sorcery (Tradition) or Minor Sorcery. Species such as the Hutts and the Yuuzhan Vong are known for this quality.

### Armoured Adept
**Max:** Toughness Bonus · **Tests:** Use the Force while wearing armour

Most Force users find armour a barrier between themselves and the Force; you have learned to work through it. Reduce the penalty of −1 SL per Armour Point on your most-armoured location by your rank. The Jensaarai, the Mandalorian Force users and the armoured Sith of the Great War all trained this way.

### Code (Tradition)
**Max:** 1 · **Tests:** Cool Tests and Tests to regain Resolve while you keep your code

*Placeholder until the tradition pages and Force Influence are settled.* You live by your tradition's code: the Jedi Code, the Sith Code, the Resol'nare of the Mandalorians, or another agreed with the GM. While you keep it you gain +1 SL on successful Cool Tests and regain 1 extra Resolve whenever you act on your Motivation. When you break it, you lose both benefits until you have made amends in a way your tradition recognises, and the GM may award Dark Side Influence for the breach.

### Wild Talent
**Max:** 1

The Force moves in you, but no one ever taught you what to do with it. Choose one universal power from any discipline; you use it, and up to Willpower Bonus minor effects agreed with the GM, by testing Willpower at −10 instead of Use the Force. Any double on such a Test, success or failure, gives you a point of Dark Side Influence as the raw Force answers your need with its own. You may not take Force Sense, Force Control or Force Alter until a tradition has trained you, at which point you buy Force Sensitive (Tradition) and this talent is replaced by the first rank of the matching discipline at no cost. Kyp Durron, the young Asajj Ventress and the outcasts of Dathomir began this way.

### Force Sorcery (Tradition)
**Max:** 1

You practise one of the sorcerous traditions of the Force: Sith sorcery, the magicks of the Nightsisters or the Singing Mountain Clan of Dathomir, Krath alchemy, or another agreed with the GM. You may now memorise spells from your tradition's list, using the sorcery rules, for the XP cost below, and may learn rituals your tradition teaches. You may normally know only one sorcerous tradition. You may unlearn this talent for 100 XP, losing every spell you know.

| Spells currently known | XP cost for a new spell |
| --- | --- |
| up to Intelligence Bonus × 1 | 100 XP |
| up to Intelligence Bonus × 2 | 200 XP |
| up to Intelligence Bonus × 3 | 300 XP |
| up to Intelligence Bonus × 4 | 400 XP |

So a sorcerer with Intelligence Bonus 4 pays 100 XP for each of the first four spells, 200 XP for the next four, and so on.

### Dark Side Sorcery
**Max:** Number of spells available

By accident or design you have given part of yourself to the dark side, and it has taught you something in return. Each time you take this talent you gain one spell from the dark side list, and one point of Dark Side Influence as the knowledge settles into your mind, never to be forgotten. The Sith holocrons and the tombs of Korriban are the usual teachers.

### Minor Sorcery
**Max:** 1

You have the spark of sorcery and the first rudiments of control. On taking this talent you permanently memorise a number of minor spells equal to your Willpower Bonus. Further minor spells cost XP as below.

| Minor spells currently known | XP cost for a new spell |
| --- | --- |
| up to Willpower Bonus × 1 | 50 XP |
| up to Willpower Bonus × 2 | 100 XP |
| up to Willpower Bonus × 3 | 150 XP |
| up to Willpower Bonus × 4 | 200 XP |

### Force Attunement
**Max:** Initiative Bonus · **Tests:** Use the Force when channelling

Training or raw talent lets you gather the Force for a spell more safely than most. You do not suffer a Miscast when you roll a double on a successful Use the Force Test made to channel the Force for sorcery.

### Fluent Incantation
**Max:** Initiative Bonus · **Tests:** Language (The Force) when casting

The words and gestures of your tradition's sorcery come to you without effort, however complex the working. You do not suffer a Miscast when you roll a double on a successful Language (The Force) Test.

### Sense Sorcery
**Max:** Initiative Bonus · **Tests:** Intuition Tests to detect sorcerers

You can feel the residue that sorcery leaves on those who practise it. Whenever you meet a Force sorcerer you may attempt an Average (+20) Intuition Test; if you pass, you know that the target works sorcery, though not which tradition.

### Ritual Ward
**Max:** Willpower Bonus · **Tests:** Use the Force when raising a ward

*Placeholder until the Force sorcery and tradition pages are written.* You know the old rites that turn harm aside: Dathomiri protection spells, Sith alchemical wards, the Jal Shey charms. With ten minutes' ritual and a successful Use the Force Test you lay a ward on yourself or one willing being that lasts until the next sunrise; it counts as Armour Points equal to your rank against Force powers and against one physical attack of the warded being's choice, after which it is spent.
