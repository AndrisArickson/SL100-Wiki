# Careers

A character's '''career''' is what they do for a living and how they grow: a class, a career of four levels, an Advance Scheme that says which characteristics each level opens, and at every level the Status, Skills, Talents and Trappings that come with it. Level 1 lists 8 skills, level 2 adds 6, level 3 adds 4 and level 4 adds 2; every level grants 4 talents. Completing a level takes 5, 10, 15 or 20 Advances in each of its characteristics and in 8 of its skills, plus one of its talents. Changing career costs 100 XP if the current level is complete, 200 if not, and 100 more to change class. Status tiers are still written Brass / Silver / Gold n; renaming them is an open decision.

**Status tags.** `[sl100]` marks the 24 careers written by the design group. Their skill, talent and trapping names were converted to the SL100 lists on 12 Oct 2026 (Consume Alcohol -> Consume Intoxicant, Heal -> Medicine, Read/Write -> Datapad Use, Halberd -> Force Pike and so on); every line that was changed ends `[converted]` and any leftover placeholder is marked `[review: placeholder]`. `[review]` marks the 65 careers added on 12 Oct 2026: 43 converted from WFRP 4e core careers, 16 converted from the WFRP supplements you own (Up in Arms: Gunner, Officer, Armoured Knight with its Orders note, Sharpshooter, Elite Guard, Line Trooper, Camp Follower, Freelance, Beast Rider, Navigator; Sea of Claws: Beachcomber; Archives of the Empire: Beast Handler, Shadow Scout, Fieldwarden, Clan Ranger, Hired Gun), and 6 Star Wars-native with no parent (Pilot, Slicer, Mechanic, Politician, Gambler, Holonet Journalist, plus Pirate, Marine and Martial Artist designed in WFRP's shape). Each review career ends with Conversion notes listing what was changed or proposed; in particular the Advance Scheme levels 2-4 of every converted career are proposals, because only the level-1 characteristics could be read from the rulebook text. Known conflicts between careers and with the other chapters are collected in docs/rules-review/careers-conflicts.md.

**Classes.** Academics, Citizens, Dignitaries, Force Wielders, Freelancers, Scoundrels, Spacers (new: starship crews, dock and customs work, salvage and piracy, replacing WFRP's Riverfolk) and Warriors.

## Academics

### Advocate

![Advocate (SL100 original art)](File:SL100 Career Advocate.jpg)

`[review]`

*You argue galactic law in courtrooms, corporate boardrooms and Senate committee chambers.*

Advocates are the lawyers of the galaxy: trained in Republic statute, Imperial decree and the contract codes of the great corporations. Most study at a Core World law faculty or clerk for a chartered advocate on Coruscant, then sell their counsel to merchants, nobles and guilds. Under the Empire, the law is whatever the Moff says it is, and the clever Advocate learns to work the system rather than fight it; under the Republic and in the courts of Corellia or Alderaan, a sharp Advocate can bring down a senator. Some end up as magistrates with the power of sentence over a whole sector.

### Engineer

![Engineer (SL100 original art)](File:SL100 Career Engineer.jpg)

`[review]`

*You design, build and keep running the starships, droids and machines that hold the galaxy together.*

Engineers are the formally trained builders of the galaxy: starship drive systems, droid chassis, planetary shield generators, siege weaponry and the cannon mounts on a corvette's hull. They train at Corellian and Kuati shipyard academies, in the engineering corps of the Republic and Imperial navies, or under a licensed master in a Mid Rim guild workshop. Every faction wants them: the Empire for its war machine, the Rebellion for keeping salvaged ships flying, the Hutts for whatever pays. A chartered Engineer with a guild permit commands real respect in the Core, while a self-taught one on the Rim is a mechanic with ambitions.

### Pharmacist

![Pharmacist (SL100 original art)](File:SL100 Career Pharmacist.jpg)

`[review]`

*You brew the stims, bacta compounds and poisons that keep the galaxy's bodies running, and sometimes stop them.*

Pharmacists compound medicines, stims, antitoxins and bacta derivatives for medcenters, starship sickbays and back-alley clinics alike. Trained at Core World pharmacological institutes or apprenticed to a licensed chemist on a mid-rim trade world, they hold the knowledge that sits between a healer and a poisoner, and many quietly work both sides of that line. Under the Republic their guild permits are respected; under the Empire the same permits are scrutinised for spice trafficking, and a Pharmacist on the Outer Rim is as likely to supply a Hutt kajidic as a planetary hospital. Rebel cells prize them for field antitoxins and black-market bacta.

### Physician (Medic)

![Physician (Medic) (SL100 original art)](File:SL100 Career Physician Medic.jpg)

`[sl100]`
*With a steady hand and a well-stocked medpac, you practice the art of healing, striving to save lives across the galaxy.*

Medics study patients' vitals, prescribe stims and surgeries, and patch the wounded together with bacta and synthflesh. The formal science of galactic medicine is well-established, but on backwater worlds and Outer Rim outposts supplies are scarce, leaving practitioners reliant on improvisation. Medicine's reputation has suffered from black-market "miracle cures" peddled by chem-pushers that do nothing or cause outright harm. Medics learn their profession at a Core World medical academy or apprenticed to a chartered surgical-droid programme. Most cheap surgery in the underlevels is undertaken by back-alley practitioners whose training is informal. Trained doctors with strong stomachs are in constant demand by mercenary outfits, planetary militias, and starship crews. The most famed Medics almost exclusively tend to corporate executives, senators, and the nobility of the Core Worlds.

### Scholar

![Scholar (SL100 original art)](File:SL100 Career Scholar.jpg)

`[review]`

*You are an archivist, historian or xenobiologist, hunting knowledge across the galaxy's libraries and ruins.*

Scholars are the academics of the galaxy: historians of the Old Republic, xenobiologists cataloguing Rim species, archivists of the Jedi Temple library or the University of Coruscant. They study at Core World universities, in the great archives of Obroa-skai, or under a professor who needs a cheap research assistant. Under the Empire, scholarship is politically dangerous: histories are rewritten, the Jedi are erased, and the Imperial Security Bureau reads dissertations. The Rebellion recruits Scholars to interpret captured data; the Empire recruits them to catalogue what it has looted. A professor with a chair at a Core university is a minor celebrity; a field scholar on Ossus or Yavin IV is a treasure hunter with a degree.

### Slicer

![Slicer (SL100 original art)](File:SL100 Career Slicer.jpg)

`[review]`

*You break into the galaxy's computers, droids and security networks, and sell what you find.*

Slicers are the intrusion specialists of the HoloNet age, cracking datavaults, rewriting droid memories and spoofing Imperial code cylinders for whoever pays. Most learn their trade in the lower levels of Coruscant, Nar Shaddaa or a corporate cubicle farm before going freelance, and the best are courted by Rebel cells, Hutt kajidics and Imperial Intelligence alike. During the Clone Wars they fought a shadow war inside Separatist and Republic networks; under the Empire, COMPNOR hunts them as subversives. A seasoned Slicer never meets a client in person and never uses the same terminal twice.

### Agitator

![Agitator (SL100 original art)](File:SL100 Career Agitator.jpg)

`[sl100]`
*Charismatic and articulate, you champion your cause in the face of Imperial opposition.*

Agitators lobby for political causes using HoloNet broadcasts, illicit data-leaflets, and public speaking in spaceport plazas. They muster the down-trodden populace's sympathy and support but must be wary of drawing the attention of ISB sympathisers and corporate enforcers intent on maintaining the established order. The most dangerous Agitators have destabilised the rule of Moffs, planetary governors, and even entire sectors. Pamphleteers slice anti-Imperial slogans into public datafeeds, or distribute leaflets in market districts, though often those they seek to reach cannot read Aurebesh. Religious Agitators can make a good living as street preachers of the Force or other creeds, receiving donations from pious devotees and attracting zealots as followers. Agitators who survive long enough to become Demagogues are often supported by powerful, hidden allies — Rebel cells, rival syndicates, sympathetic senators — pursuing change for their own motives.

### Force Shadow

![Force Shadow (SL100 original art)](File:SL100 Career Force Shadow.jpg)

`[sl100]`
**JEDI SENTINEL**

You are the Jedi the Order sends when it cannot afford to be seen sending anyone. Not a warrior, not a diplomat — something harder to define and far more dangerous. The Sentinel walks the space between light and shadow, fluent in the languages of both. While Guardians train their bodies and Consulars train their minds, you have trained your instincts: when to trust, when to doubt, when to act, and when to vanish.

The Order has always needed people like you, even when it has been uncomfortable admitting it. Sentinels root out dark side cults before they metastasise into open war. They go undercover in the Hutt cartels, the Black Sun, the Mandalorian clans. They track Force-sensitive children across the Outer Rim before the Sith can find them first. They sit in cantinas on Nar Shaddaa nursing the same drink for three hours, waiting. Some Sentinels work in close coordination with the Council. Others are given a target, a comm frequency to check in on, and nothing else. A few stop checking in altogether — not because they have fallen, but because the work demands it.

The galaxy does not remember Sentinels. That is precisely the point.

**Force Tradition (Force Phantom)**

The Force Phantom can actively project a quality of unremarkableness, becoming the person no one quite remembers, operating to support the interest of their tradition across the galaxy, embracing technology and self reliance, they regard their gear not as a crutch but as an extension of their traditions principle of preparation — sensing what is coming is only useful if you have already prepared the appropriate response. The Force Shadow fights ahead of the moment, never in it. By the time violence arrives they have already felt its shape forming and positioned themselves accordingly, their movement is simply the last step in a process that began long before anyone else in the room understood there was a process at all. Force enhancement makes them briefly, terrifyingly fast at close range, before their opponent has finished deciding what to do next. They favour precision over power, stillness over aggression, and endings over engagements. If you have seen a Force Shadow fight, something has already gone wrong with their plan — and even then, you are unlikely to see it twice.

**SITH INQUISITOR (Dark Side Jedi Sentinel)**

You are the most dangerous thing in any room you enter, and no one realises it until it is far too late. Where the Sith Warrior announces themselves with the crack of a lightsaber and the stench of ozone, you arrive quietly — asking questions in a tone that sounds almost reasonable, right up until the moment it doesn't. The dark side flows through you not as a battering ram but as a scalpel, and you have learned that a scalpel, applied correctly, can do things a battering ram never could.The Sith Inquisitor is the Empire's blade in the dark. You are sent to find heresy within the hierarchy, to break the minds of prisoners that brute force has failed to crack, to uncover ancient Sith knowledge buried on dead worlds and determine whether it is useful or simply dangerous.
